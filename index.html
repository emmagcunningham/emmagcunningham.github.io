Emma G. Cunningham

I study the cognitive processes that underlie planning, with a focus on how video games and mini-games can serve as tools for measuring and strengthening higher-order cognitive skills. I'm a PhD candidate in the Learning and Transfer Lab at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, advised by Professor C. Shawn Green, and a graduate student associate in the Brain and Learning Lab at the University of Geneva, advised by Professor Daphné Bavelier.

Alongside research, I teach. I've been an instructor and teaching assistant for courses ranging from a 200-student introductory lecture to a 24-student capstone seminar, and my teaching centers on transparency, individualized support, and helping students see themselves as capable researchers and learners.

I hold an M.S. in Psychology from UW–Madison (2023) and a B.Sc. in Cognitive Science, with an emphasis in HCI & AI, from UC Santa Cruz (2021). I'm on the academic job market this cycle.

Research

My research asks how we plan — and how well existing tools actually measure that ability. A lot of my work uses games as an experimental format: they let researchers observe planning behavior directly, in a setting that's engaging enough to sustain real cognitive effort. Recent projects include:

  • Building and validating a novel game-based paradigm for assessing planning abilities.
  • A critical analysis of common planning-ability measurements, examining how well established metrics agree with one another.
  • Studying individual differences in learning a complex visuomotor task.
  • Meta-analytic and replication work on digital cognitive-training interventions, including reading interventions and attention training.
