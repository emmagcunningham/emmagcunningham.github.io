Emma G. Cunningham

I study the cognitive processes underlying planning, using video games as tools to measure and strengthen higher-order cognitive skills. I'm a PhD candidate in Psychology at UW–Madison (advised by C. Shawn Green and Daphné Bavelier). I am set to defend my dissertation titled "Deconstructing Planning: An Examination of Planning Measurement Through Novel Game-Based Paradigms" in May 2027

In addition to conducting research, I am passionate about providing inclusive, inspiring and practical teaching to students of all backgrounds and educational levels. I've been an instructor and teaching assistant for courses ranging from a 100-student introductory courses in Intro Psychology and Research Methods, to a 24-student capstone seminar. My teaching centers on transparency, individualized support, and helping students see themselves as capable learners and critical thinkers. I currently hold an M.S. (UW–Madison, 2023) and B.Sc. in Cognitive Science (UC Santa Cruz, 2021), plan to complete my PhD by May 2027, and I'm on the academic job market this cycle.

Mentorship

Undergraduate students are integral collaborators in my research program — I've mentored over 50 students across every stage of the research process, several of whom have gone on to graduate and professional programs. My memtorship focuses on cultivating independent thinking skills, self confidence, and curiosity. I respect and encourage students' autonomy to determine the style of mentorship they want from me, while providing scaffolded, and hands-on support throughout. My students develop skills they can use in whatever area they are interested in right from the beginning, and we work together to find a path that is best suited to prepare them to achieve their goals.

Research

My research asks how we plan — and how well existing tools actually measure that ability. A lot of my work uses games as an experimental format: they let researchers observe planning behavior directly, in a setting that's engaging enough to sustain real cognitive effort. Recent projects include:

  • Building and validating a novel game-based paradigm for assessing planning abilities.
  • A critical analysis of common planning-ability measurements, examining how well established metrics agree with one another.
  • Studying individual differences in learning a complex visuomotor task.
  • Meta-analytic and replication work on digital cognitive-training interventions, including reading interventions and attention training.
